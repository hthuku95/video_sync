About charging $300/month

I wouldn't start by asking whether $300/month is too high. I'd ask whether your tool creates more than $300/month in value for them.

Imagine a professional clipper:

They produce 20–50 clips a day.
Each clip might take 10–20 minutes to find, edit, caption, brand, and export.
If your agent reduces that to 2–3 minutes of review and approval, it could save several hours every day.

If someone earns thousands of dollars a month from clipping, paying $300/month could be entirely reasonable if your tool is reliable.

On the other hand, if your customer is:

only clipping part-time,
producing 5–10 clips a week,
or just experimenting,

then $300/month is likely too expensive.

I would validate pricing before locking it in

Rather than saying:

"It's $300/month."

How many clips do you make per day?
What's the slowest part of your workflow?
If this worked perfectly, what would it be worth to you?
Would you pay for it monthly, or would you prefer a pay-per-clip model?

Those answers are often more valuable than guessing a price.
